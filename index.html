Talia Baddour

I am a PhD Candidate advised by Dr. Brian Aguado at the iBiomaterials Lab at UC San Diego with six years of research experience spanning computational modeling, biomaterials engineering, and single-cell and spatial genomics. My expertise spans cardiovascular biology, fibrosis and calcification, epigenetics, and sex differences in disease. I specialize in generating and analyzing single-cell and spatial transcriptomic data from human tissue to uncover sex-specific disease mechanisms. I bridge tissue-level omics approaches with 3D hydrogel disease models to uncover clinically relevant mechanisms of heart disease. Broadly, my goal is to translate multi-omic insights into precision therapeutics.

Research

My research combines Single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and engineered biomaterials to investigate sex differences in calcific aortic valve disease and cardiac fibrosis.

Spatial and Single-Cell Mapping Reveals Valvular Interstitial Cell and Macrophage Sex Differences in Calcific Aortic Valve Disease
Talia Baddour, Van K. Ninh, Rayyan M. Gorashi, Rene Peña, Kevin R. King, and Brian A. Aguado.
Arteriosclerosis, Thrombosis, and Vascular Biology, 2026  

I utilized Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics to build sex-specific cell atlases of human aortic valves from patients with calcific aortic valve disease. I identified male and female valvular interstitial cell (VIC) populations near sites of calcification and mapped the spatial organization of myofibroblast- and osteoblast-associated genes across the valve. I also characterized sex differences macrophage - pathogenic VIC crosstalk, giving insight into how biological sex shapes cell-cell interactions that drive valve calcification.
Y Chromosome–Linked UTY Modulates Sex Differences in Valvular Fibroblast Methylation in Response to Nanoscale Extracellular Matrix Cues
Rayyan M. Gorashi, Talia Baddour, Sarah J. Chittle, Nicole E. Félix Vélez, Michaela A. Wenning, Kristi S. Anseth, Luisa Mestroni, Brisa Peña, Peng Guo, and Brian A. Aguado.
Science Advances, 2025  

I performed single-cell sequencing on human aortic valve tissue from patients with aortic valve stenosis and found up-regulated pro-osteogenic genes and decreased expression of the Y chromosome–linked histone demethylase UTY in male valvular interstitial cells (VICs). We engineered a hydrogel culture platform with embedded nanoparticles to probe how nanoscale matrix cues regulate sex-specific methylation states in VICs. UTY knockdown increased methylation and promoted an osteoblast-like phenotype uniquely in male VICs, revealing a previously unidentified role for UTY in regulating calcification in males.
Sex Chromosome and Gonadal Regulation of Cardiac Myofibroblast Activation Decoupled Using Mechanically Tunable Hydrogels and the Four Core Genotypes Model
Rayyan M. Gorashi, Talia Baddour, Kati Richter, Ashley K. Nguyen, Mason N. Faust, Meaghan C. Loud, Sarah L. Broders, Qingxin Zheng, Lukas Graner, Xuqi Chen, Arthur P. Arnold, and Brian A. Aguado.
bioRxiv (preprint), 2026  

We used the Four Core Genotypes (FCG) mouse model to decouple the contributions of sex chromosome complement and gonadal sex to cardiac myofibroblast activation. On poly(ethylene glycol) hydrogels mimicking healthy and fibrotic myocardium, FCG cardiac fibroblasts showed sex chromosome contributions to αlpha smooth muscle actin stress fiber formation and YAP1 expression, along with distinct gonadal and chromosomal sex-biased protein secretion profiles. I performed Single-cell RNA sequencing on FCG mouse cardiac fibroblasts. Additionally I wrote an analysis pipeline which revealed sex chromosome- vs. gonad-biased expression of transcriptional regulators, cytokines, and growth factors in cardiac fibroblasts.
